# Recording your presentation video

Your presentation video should be about 5 minutes at most. You can hand it in as a file (.mp4 or .mov, up to 250MB) or as a link to the video - just make sure the link works and can be opened by your tutor.

You need to show both your slides and your live portfolio site in the browser, with your webcam (headshot) visible. The simplest way is to record your whole screen and your webcam at the same time, and switch between your slides and the browser as you present.

You can make your slides in any tool you like (PowerPoint, Google Slides, Keynote, Canva). Whichever recorder you use, record your **entire screen** (not just one window or tab), and if you have more than one monitor, keep your slides and browser on the same screen.

## Placing your headshot

- Put your webcam image in one of the **lower corners** (left or right). Its shape does not matter.
- Pick the corner so that you face slightly **into** the screen, towards the middle. If you are turned a little to the right, use the bottom-left corner, and vice versa.
- Keep it small enough that it does not cover important content, such as buttons or text you are demonstrating.

## Recommended: Clipchamp (Mac and Windows)

Clipchamp is free from Microsoft. It runs in the browser on Mac and Windows, and is also available as an app on Windows. You need a (free) Microsoft account.

> **How you get your headshot:** Clipchamp records your screen and your webcam as **two separate clips**, placed on top of each other. You may not see yourself on screen while recording - you place the headshot in a lower corner **afterwards**, in the editor (step 4).

1. Start a new video, open **Record & create**, and choose **Screen and camera**. Allow access to your camera and microphone.
2. Select your **entire screen**, then start recording. Present, switching between your slides and the browser demo as you go.
3. Stop the recording. The screen and camera recordings are added to your timeline as separate clips.
4. In the preview, select the camera image and drag and resize it into a lower corner.
5. **Export**. Choose 1080p, or 720p for a smaller file. The file is saved as .mp4.

## Mac alternative: QuickTime and the Screenshot toolbar

Built into macOS - no account or install needed.

> **How you get your headshot:** the QuickTime camera window **is** your headshot. It is just a window on your screen showing your webcam, and the screen recording captures it along with everything else. What you see in that corner while recording is exactly what ends up in the video.

1. Open **QuickTime Player** and choose **File > New Movie Recording**, then **View > Float on Top**. Make the camera window smaller and drag it into a lower corner. Place it carefully - it cannot be moved after you have recorded.
2. Press **Cmd+Shift+5**, choose **Record Entire Screen**, and under **Options** select your microphone. Click **Record**.
3. Present, switching between slides and the browser. The camera window stays on top.
4. Stop with the stop button in the menu bar. The .mov file is saved to your Desktop.
5. If the file is larger than 250MB, open it in QuickTime and use **File > Export As > 1080p** (or 720p).

## Link option: Loom (Mac and Windows)

Loom records your screen and webcam together, and gives you a link to share. On the **free plan you only get a link** - you cannot download the video as a file - and recordings stop automatically at 5 minutes.

> **How you get your headshot:** Loom shows your webcam as a **bubble on your screen**, and the recording captures it along with everything else. Drag it into a lower corner before you start - what you see is what ends up in the video.

1. Install the Loom desktop app (or the Chrome extension) and sign up for a free account.
2. Choose **Screen and Camera**, select **Full screen**, and pick your camera and microphone.
3. Click **Start recording**. Your webcam appears as a bubble you can drag into a lower corner. Present, switching between your slides and the browser.
4. Stop the recording. The video opens in your browser.
5. Check that the video can be viewed by anyone with the link, then copy the link and submit it.

## What about PowerPoint and Canva?

- **PowerPoint (Record)** and **Canva (Present and record)** only record your slides, not your browser. To show your live site, you would have to record it separately and insert the clip on a slide. Use Clipchamp, QuickTime or Loom instead unless you are comfortable with that extra step.

## Before you record

- Do a 20-second test recording to check sound, camera and screen.
- Close unrelated tabs and apps, and turn on Do Not Disturb (Mac) or Focus (Windows).
- Have your portfolio and project demos open and loaded in the browser.
- Check the length (about 5 minutes) and, if you upload a file, the size (up to 250MB). If you submit a link, open it in a private browser window to make sure it works without logging in.
